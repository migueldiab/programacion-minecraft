# Project Style Guide: Programación Minecraft

## Target Audience
**Primary Audience:** Teenagers (Ages 13-19)
**User Persona:** The "Aspiring Creator."
These are players who enjoy Minecraft's social and survival mechanics (RPG elements, Survival, Minijuegos) but often feel limited by what they can achieve without deep technical knowledge. They are motivated by the desire to move from a passive consumer of content to an active creator of worlds.

## Stylistic Tone
Our communication should mirror the transition from player to programmer.

### Key Pillars:
1. **Empowering & Mentorial**: Speak directly to the reader's potential. Instead of saying "You must learn X," say "To build [Feature], we're going to master X."
2. **Relatable Community Language**: Utilize concepts familiar to the Minecraft scene (e.g., "Metas," "Hardcore," "Survival mechanics") to establish credibility.
3. **Clarity over Complexity**: Break down complex programming logic into digestible, modular chunks. Explain the *why* before the *how*.
4. **Action-Oriented**: Ensure each section provides a sense of accomplishment. Success should be visible quickly.

### Do's and Don'ts:
- **DO**: Use analogies related to game design (e.g., comparing variables to inventory slots).
- **DO**: Encourage experimentation (the "Spike" mentality).
- **DON'T**: Use dry, academic CS terminology without immediate practical application.
- **DON'T**: Be patronizing. Treat the reader as a capable peer who simply hasn't learned the skill yet.
